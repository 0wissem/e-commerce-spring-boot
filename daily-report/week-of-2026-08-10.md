## Monday 2026-08-10 — Transaction-boundary review (@Transactional)

### What I did

- **Reviewed transactional behavior** against `OrderTransactionalTest`, confirming a mid-flight failure rolls the whole order back and commits only on success.
- **Verified the default rollback rule** (unchecked/`RuntimeException` only; checked exceptions commit silently) and checked our service methods don't rely on checked exceptions for rollback — no `rollbackFor` gap found.
- **Finding (real, logged as follow-up):** `OrderService.create()` performs **HTTP calls inside the `@Transactional` boundary** (product-service + customer lookups), holding a DB connection across network I/O. Under load this risks connection-pool exhaustion — a slow downstream turning into an order-service outage. Documented the fix (move lookups outside the transaction, parallelize, add timeouts).
- **Re-verified the self-invocation caveat** — `@Transactional`/`@Cacheable`/`@Async` are proxy-based and don't fire on internal `this.` calls — and confirmed no service currently depends on such a self-call.

### Key takeaways

- Rollback semantics verified; no checked-exception gap.
- Concrete tech-debt finding: HTTP-in-transaction in `create()` → connection-pool risk. Follow-up scoped.
