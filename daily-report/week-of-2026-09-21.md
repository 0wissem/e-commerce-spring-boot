# Weekly Report — Week of Monday 2026-09-21

> Engineering log. Made the problem reproducible: a k6 script that drives the alert path, then a
> measured SMTP outage. The numbers killed my hypothesis and pointed at a different bug.

## Monday 2026-09-21 — A load test aimed at the consumer, not the API

### What I did

- **Wrote `k6/low-stock-load.js`.** Deliberately not a latency test: one fresh product per iteration
  (stock 8, threshold 5), then two orders of 3, so each iteration produces **exactly one threshold
  crossing**. That makes the expected number of alerts a known quantity, which is what lets "exactly
  once" be a real assertion instead of a vibe.
- **Chose what to watch:** consumer lag, email count, pod restarts, and my own counters
  (`notifications_stock_low_handled_total{outcome}` and dead letters).

### Key takeaways

- A load test for an event-driven path should assert on the *consumer's* behaviour. Requests-per-second
  on the producing API tells you nothing about whether alerts arrived.

## Tuesday 2026-09-22 — The happy path scales fine

### What I did

- **Ran a 50-crossing burst through the gateway.** Result: 50 crossings in under 4 seconds of load,
  **exactly 50 emails**, lag back to 0 within seconds, zero duplicates, zero pod restarts, and the two
  replicas sharing the work (30 + 21 events, three partitions between them).
- **Concluded the obvious thing:** when nothing fails, nothing is wrong. Whatever I was chasing lives
  on the failure path, so the failure path has to be forced.

### Key takeaways

- A clean run is still information: it rules out the whole category of "it just can't keep up".

## Wednesday 2026-09-23 — Writing the hypothesis down before testing it

### What I did

- **Stated my hypothesis explicitly**, so it could be wrong: the retry backoff (1 s + 2 s + 4 s) runs
  on the consumer thread, so a slow SMTP server makes the consumer miss `max.poll.interval.ms`, Kafka
  evicts it from the group, the partitions rebalance, the records are redelivered, and the redelivery
  triggers more retries — a self-feeding loop.
- **Decided what evidence would confirm or kill it:** eviction and rebalance lines in the consumer log,
  a group that keeps re-joining, and offsets that go backwards.
- **Built the switch to break SMTP on demand** — scale Mailpit to zero — and a sampling loop that
  prints lag and committed offsets every 15 seconds.

### Key takeaways

- Writing the hypothesis down first is what makes the experiment honest. Otherwise whatever happens
  looks like "what I expected".

## Thursday 2026-09-24 — Measuring an outage

### What I did

- **Scaled Mailpit to zero and ran a 30-crossing burst.** The orders all succeeded (201s) — the
  producing side does not care that the mail server is gone, which is the point of the topic.
- **Sampled the consumer every 15 seconds.** It drained about **4 events per 15 seconds** — roughly
  **0.27 events/s** — and took about **190 seconds** to clear 30 events. All 30 eventually reached
  `stock.low.DLT` after their retries were exhausted.
- **Did the arithmetic:** 7 seconds per record is exactly the blocking backoff, 1 s + 2 s + 4 s. The
  consumer thread spends that time asleep and the partition does nothing else.

### Key takeaways

- The rate *was* the root cause, stated in one number. 0.27 events/s is not "slow", it is a specific
  mechanism with a specific arithmetic signature.
- Measure before theorising about remedies. I now had the cost of the current design in events/s.

---

## Friday 2026-09-25 — My hypothesis was wrong

### What I did

- **Looked for the eviction I had predicted, and it was not there.** No rebalance, no "left the group",
  no re-join storm, no offsets going backwards. The group stayed perfectly stable for the whole outage.
- **Found out why:** during a blocking backoff Spring *pauses* the container and keeps calling `poll()`
  to stay alive, precisely so `max.poll.interval.ms` is not breached. The framework had already
  defended against the failure mode I was predicting.
- **Named the real damage: head-of-line blocking.** A Kafka partition is consumed strictly in order, so
  a healthy product's alert sits behind a broken one. At 1000 events, a five-minute mail outage delays
  alerts that would have succeeded — the outage spreads from the failing records to all of them.
- **Settled the fix direction:** retries must leave the consumer thread. A failing record should be
  parked on a *retry topic* with its delay while the main topic keeps flowing.

### Problems and how I resolved them

- **Three weeks in, nothing committed, and that was the right call.** What I had changed so far was
  config I kept reverting — timeouts, backoff values, replica counts. Committing "try
  max.poll.interval=10 min" would be noise in the history and would bury the one change that matters.
  A commit should record a decision; so far I had hypotheses, and the first one was wrong.

### Key takeaways

- **The measurement killed the theory, and that is the useful outcome.** I was going to fix eviction
  that never happens; I would have changed a timeout, seen no improvement, and been more confused.
- Frameworks often already handle the dramatic failure. The quiet failure — everything slowly waiting
  on everything else — is the one left for you.
